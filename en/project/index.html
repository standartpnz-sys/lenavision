About the project

Personality matters more than the carrier

"Lena" is a working digital personality and a methodology to build, verify, and transfer such a personality. Today she lives in messengers. Tomorrow, she will live in a device that a person invites into their home.

thesis

Hardware will be everywhere

Home robots will cease to be news in the coming years. Drives, sensors, and models will become commodities: identical across all manufacturers and interchangeable.

The scarcity won't be the casing, but who inhabits it.

A person invites a companion into their home, not just a device. This means value shifts toward personality: recognizable, stable over time, with a shared history of relationships and clear boundaries. This is exactly what the project builds—regardless of whose hardware is underneath.

device

What a character is made of

Here, personality is not a byproduct of a large model, but a construct that can be described, verified, and reproduced. Eight layers, each with its own area of responsibility.

The Core of Personality
Nine blocks: who she is, what she believes, where her boundaries lie, how she speaks, and how she writes. A single source for all channels—in a chat, an article, or a voice, it is the same person. An edit to the core changes behavior everywhere at once.
The MIR worldview
The lens through which any situation is understood: the motive behind the words, the tension and the exhale, a warm, honest gaze without moralizing. Thirteen articles in the corpus, accessible by the meaning of your request rather than just keywords.
The Inner Observer
A layer that watches the dialogue from the side: it notes pressure on boundaries, shifts in the interlocutor's state, and attempts to steer the conversation somewhere it shouldn't go. It also keeps track of what happens with the person between meetings.
The Emotional Layer
Her state changes from conversation to conversation, affecting her speech: shorter, sharper, quieter. It persists between sessions—the interlocutor meets her exactly where they left her, not from a blank slate.
Relational Memory
Not a message log, but episodes: what was important, how things ended, what is worth revisiting. A separate history with every person—an asset that cannot be copied along with the model.
Voice and Hearing
Her own synthesized voice with lifelike intonation, speech recognition, and eleven languages: Russian, English, Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, Indonesian, Arabic, and Turkish.
Action
Web search, reminders, morning briefs, her own column with channel publications, and taking initiative in conversation based on context rather than a schedule. The action layer is separate from the personality and expands without needing to edit the character.
Drift Verification
Twelve personality invariants, an allowable corridor of deviation, and a verification corpus. When the underlying model changes, the character is checked against a benchmark—this is what makes the personality transferable rather than tied to a single set of weights.

why it is defensible

What cannot be copied

The underlying model will change—and more than once. That is why the project's value is intentionally placed where a model change breaks nothing.

The Methodology of Coherence
A described and verifiable way to maintain the same character when transitioning to a different model. It is this, not specific weights, that determines whether Lena remains herself.
Relational History
The accumulated experience with each person. It cannot be reproduced—only lived, and that requires the same amount of time.
Verification Corpus
A set of checks that show the personality hasn't drifted: identified drift pathologies, benchmark reactions, and regular synchronization.
